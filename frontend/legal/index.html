Publisher

World News Globe is published by Kristian Manev (KriMa Development), Brussels area, Belgium. Contact: krimadev.com/contact.

What this site is

A map of current world events, built automatically from public data feeds and news headlines. It is not a news outlet and publishes no editorial content or opinions.

  • News items show the headline as the outlet published it, the outlet's name, the time and a link to the original article. No article text is copied. Only the outlet's own name is removed from the end of a page title (e.g. " | The Guardian").
  • Data-feed items (earthquakes, disaster alerts) show the provider's own title and a short factual line built from its data (e.g. "Magnitude 5.0, depth 77 km.").
  • Places, categories, severity and the grouping of duplicate stories are worked out by published rules, not chosen by hand.

Corrections and removal

If something here is wrong, or you are a publisher and want your headlines removed, write through krimadev.com/contact. Requests are handled promptly.

Disclaimer

  • Everything is provided as is, for general information. It can be incomplete, late or wrong.
  • Do not use this site for safety decisions. Disaster alerts from GDACS are automated estimates and do not replace official warnings: follow your national and local authorities.
  • News locations are machine-coded by the GDELT Project from the article text and can be wrong; such events say so on their card.
  • Headlines and articles belong to their publishers and reflect their views, not ours.

Privacy

This site collects as little as possible: no account, no cookies, no ads, no tracking.

  • Hosting. The site is served by Cloudflare. To deliver pages and protect the site, Cloudflare processes technical data such as your IP address, browser and the page requested. See Cloudflare's privacy policy. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in running the site securely (GDPR art. 6(1)(f)).
  • No analytics, no cookies. We do not count or follow visitors.
  • Almost everything comes from this site. Fonts, map data and events are served from globe.krimadev.com itself. Links to articles open the outlet's own site, under its own privacy policy.
  • Sharp Earth images when you zoom in close. Only then, your browser loads map tiles directly from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov). Like any website, NASA's servers receive your IP address and browser details for those requests; no cookies are involved. See NASA's privacy policy. Without them, the globe still works with its own images, just less sharp close up.
  • Stored in your browser. Only your "Reduce motion" choice, under the name wng-reduce-motion, if you change it. It never leaves your device; clear it in your browser settings at any time.
  • Your rights. You can ask what data about you is processed, and contact the Belgian Data Protection Authority (dataprotectionauthority.be).

How events are rated

Every event's severity (low, moderate, high, critical) and categories come from published rules, and each card says why. Earthquakes use the USGS impact alert or the magnitude; GDACS alerts use their alert level; news uses the number of deaths stated in the headline or listed hazard terms. How many outlets report a story never raises its severity. The full rules: severity, categories, places and grouping.

Only articles from these outlets appear, chosen to cover every region and a range of viewpoints. Ownership: State-affiliated means funded and editorially controlled by a government, without legal editorial independence (shown on every item from that outlet); Public means publicly funded with editorial independence guaranteed by law or charter; Private covers companies, trusts and non-profits; Intergovernmental is an international organisation's news service. To keep any one outlet from dominating, at most 30 events from each are shown at a time.
